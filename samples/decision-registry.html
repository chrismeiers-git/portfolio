Add a Phonetic Name field to the Person record

DR-044  ·  Approved  ·  Registrar / IR Lead  ·  June 2026
Context
Commencement announcers, ID-card printing, and the LMS's name-pronunciation feature each needed a reliable phonetic spelling for a student's name, and three different offices were maintaining their own separate lists with no shared source of truth.
Options considered
  • Leave it to each office to keep its own free-text note.
  • Add a single Phonetic Name field to the core Person record, student-editable through self-service.
  • Adopt a third-party name-pronunciation add-on tool layered on top of the SIS.
Decision
Add a single Phonetic Name field to the core Person record in Colleague, surfaced to commencement, ID printing, and the LMS.
Rationale
One field maintained once, by the student, beats three unsynced spreadsheets maintained by three different offices.

Adopt a single department-naming standard across systems

DR-047  ·  Approved  ·  IR / Data Lead  ·  July 2026
Context
Historical departments carried inconsistent full names and abbreviations across the SIS, HR, and Finance systems, which created mismatches in cross-system reporting and broke automated integration mappings.
Options considered
  • Leave legacy names as-is and reconcile mismatches case by case as they surface.
  • Adopt one standardized department-naming convention (full name plus a short code) across all systems.
  • Rename departments only inside the new SIS and leave every other system on its legacy naming.
Decision
Adopt a single standardized department-naming convention, full name plus short code, enforced across the SIS, HR, and Finance systems.
Rationale
Removes a whole category of integration-mapping errors and ambiguity in cross-system reporting, rather than patching mismatches indefinitely.

Standardize student forms on one platform

DR-048  ·  Open (pilot in progress)  ·  Program Director  ·  August 2026
Context
Dozens of paper and PDF student forms were being reproduced ad hoc across offices, each with its own routing and approval process and no shared platform.
Options considered
  • Continue with office-specific PDF forms and manual routing.
  • Adopt the newly selected forms platform as the single tool for building and approving all student forms, with automated approval routing.
  • Build custom forms individually in the university's low-code platform, office by office.
Decision
Standardize on the selected forms platform as the single home for all new and reengineered student forms, with automated routing for approvals.
Rationale
One platform for both creation and approval routing avoids duplicative tooling and keeps the vendor selection already underway from fragmenting further.
